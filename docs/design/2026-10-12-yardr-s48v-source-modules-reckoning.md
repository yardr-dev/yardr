# Source adapters as modules outside the core: a reckoning, and closing the bead

Design yardr-s48v, proposed 2026-10-12. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

A yard is one installation of yardr; a source is an outside system a hook receives from; a pack is a way of working as one directory, and a pack of kind source is an integration as one pack; a crew member is one of the yard's always-on agents; the operator is the person who runs the yard. docs/glossary.md has all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 11 Oct 2026: "github is the next one to extract". Behind that sentence stands this bead's older question: what is left of getting source adapters out of yardr's Go code and into modules outside the core, after links from a bead to an outside item and stages heard by an integration were built elsewhere. His test of any answer: a new tracker such as Jira or Asana is a pack, and no new trick in the core.

This bead is older than much of what it speaks of, so this section first says what of it is already built, what is already decided by another design, and what is left.

Already built. Linear is fully out of the Go code: yardr-ecan is merged, `internal/hooks/linear.go` is gone, and `source.BuiltIn` (internal/source/declaration.go) is `peer`, `token`, `yardr`. A declared source does everything the bead's four-part contract asked of events in and actions out: it receives webhooks, links, replies, reads, opens, sets status, moves with a stage, notifies, reports limits, and is read or updated through `yardr call` under grants (docs/integrations.md; `yardr source grant` in internal/cli/sourcecmd.go). GitHub is `packs/github` (pack.yaml, github.yaml, the normalize, thread, pr and ci scripts, SETUP.md): the declaration generalizations it needed all landed (per-hook bodies, labels as statuses, case folding, opened-since, train from the head branch; the minted credential; pull shapes and repositories; the events key with look-back reads), the pack's source has taken the name `github`, and the declared source wins every lookup when the yard has it. What the dispatcher does with a pull request and its CI is general code over declared commands (internal/dispatch/lookback.go).

Already decided by another design. "Ship packs/github, a pack of kind source shaped like packs/linear, and first teach the declaration seven general things GitHub needs that it cannot say today" (design yardr-76zs, section 3, decided by mckean 11 Oct 2026: go as proposed). "Design yardr-76zs answered what a source may declare, and it is built: GitHub and Linear are packs of kind source" (design yardr-evd6, section 2.4). "Design yardr-76zs is built: GitHub is packs/github, a pack of kind source" (design yardr-8wxe, section 2.7). "An integration is a pack and a setting, never a trick in the core for one product" (design yardr-xpf8, section 1, taken as given). Custody of credentials ("not shown unasked") is decided in yardr-76zs's open question 4 and carried further by yardr-0qs5, whose design is approved and landing. That identities are not merged across yards "was decided on 8 Oct 2026, and recorded as not to be reopened" (design yardr-r6jh, section 2.1).

What is left. Three things, none of them wanting a design from this bead. First, deleting GitHub's Go adapter now that the pack serves it: that is bead yardr-2c8b, shaped and in backlog. Second, the template that makes mckean's Jira test concrete: bead yardr-2y9m, shaped and in backlog, blocked by yardr-2c8b. Third, the one paragraph the bead always said was still to design, grants and identity per action: the module's declared actions as the crew's granted capabilities, with the scopes each action needs. Section 3 says why that paragraph should not be written here either, and the bead should close.

## 2. What exists today

The declaration reader (internal/source/declaration.go) takes every key a source module needs: `credential` with `env`, `header` and `mint`, `hook` (verify, bodies, normalize, keys), `thread` (reply, posted, read, place, open, opened, state, states, labels), `link` (host, issue, fixed, thread, fold, pull, repos), `identity` (fold), `events` (runs, head_from, pr, ci), `calls`, `notify`, `limits`. The one adapter in Go that serves every declared source is `internal/hooks/declared.go` (type Declared); commands run under one contract (one JSON object on stdin, one on stdout, 30 seconds, a credential under `credential.env` and nothing else of the yard). The compiled adapters left are two (`var Adapters` in internal/hooks/hooks.go): `github` (internal/hooks/github.go and githubapp.go, until yardr-2c8b) and `token` (internal/hooks/token.go, yardr's own, staying). What the yard does with an event is data on the hook: the says and makes tables. What a stage means in a source is the flow pack's hears map.

Grants, as built. A declared call is granted by name to a group or a crew member (`yardr source grant crm customer --crew yardmaster`; `read` grants every read call), kept as records of their own in internal/store/sourcecalls.go, asked at import and answered by the operator. Opening a thread needs `open_threads=<source>` on the crew member and setting a status needs `set_state=<source>` (internal/store/threads.go); only the operator grants either, and a stage map's move needs `set_state` too. Replying needs no grant. Identity, as built: a crew member's identity per source (`yardr crew identity`), keys folded as the declaration says; a GitHub App's id and key file mint a token narrowed to one repository through the operator's `mint_command` (contrib/github-mint), cached in memory alone. What is not data anywhere: which scopes of the service each action needs. The operator picks token permissions by hand following prose: "The token reads and writes the repository's issues and pull requests; a token that may only read answers nothing" (packs/github/SETUP.md). No declaration key names scopes, and nothing checks them.

The template does not exist yet: that is yardr-2y9m. The quiet-hook bead yardr-c98n, which "waits on this bead", wants a source the yard can read; the mechanism exists (a declared read call, made by the server under a grant: packs/github declares `calls.user`, a GET on /user), but no pack declares the "what last changed" read its idea needs.

## 3. The proposal

Close this bead with no code, because everything it asked for is built, decided elsewhere, or already owned as shaped work.

The extraction is done except for two beads that need no further design: yardr-2c8b deletes GitHub from Go in the pattern yardr-ecan set (its Done-when names the grep that proves it), and yardr-2y9m then writes the template directory with the test that holds it. After those two, mckean's test is the procedure: write a pack, change no Go. This bead adds nothing to either; their bodies quote the design that carries them (yardr-76zs) in full.

The grants-and-identity paragraph should not be written because its substance is built under the names the builders chose, and what is missing has no general mechanism and no requester. Per-action grants exist where the actions are the module's own: a declared call is granted by its name. The thread verbs are not per-module actions but the core's fixed contract (reply, read, open, state), so their grants are fixed names too (open_threads, set_state), with reply deliberately ungated so a crew member can always answer. Per-action scopes as data would need the core to check them, and no service answers "which scopes does this credential hold" in a general tongue; GitHub, Linear and the next tracker each say it differently or not at all. Where the bead's paragraph said "the module says which scopes each action needs", the module says it in SETUP.md prose, which the operator follows once. If a tracker one day needs machine-checked scopes, that is a bead of its own, filed with the tracker that needs it, on the rule design yardr-76zs set for its own left-outs. The custody half of the paragraph belongs to yardr-0qs5, whose design is approved and landing; this bead must not design it twice.

Closing ends yardr-c98n's wait in mechanism: reading a source is a declared read call, so that bead's work is to have the packs declare the read it compares against the hook's last delivery, and to teach `yardr doctor` the comparison. Its body already says as much; nothing in it needs this bead to stay open.

What a person sees after the close: `yardr bead close yardr-s48v --reason "..."` by whoever decides, and the record of the four-part contract and its dates stays on this bead and in this document. No yard changes, no command changes, no pack changes.

This holds to the yard's memories: integrations are packs and no core trick is proposed (mem-hz71); no upgrade path is offered or needed (mem-j0y0); nothing here touches networking or signalbox (mem-stdz, mem-hzpk).

## 4. Left out

Machine-checked per-action scopes, for the reason above: no general check exists, prose carries it, and no tracker has asked. Renaming open_threads and set_state into per-action grants: churn with no operator-visible gain, against preferring the mechanism that exists. A verify scheme beyond HMAC-SHA256: design yardr-76zs left it out the same way ("waits for the tracker that needs it"), and no tracker has. Anything about extraction mechanics: decided in yardr-76zs and owned by yardr-2c8b and yardr-2y9m; repeating it here would be a second design of the same work.

## 5. Open questions

1. Close this bead now, or keep it for a per-action-scopes design? Closing costs nothing: the record stays on the bead and in this document, the extraction remainder is owned by yardr-2c8b and yardr-2y9m, and custody is with yardr-0qs5. Keeping costs a design bead that waits for a requester that may never come, and risks a second design of custody beside yardr-0qs5. I would close it.

2. If it is kept: should scopes be data the core checks, or prose the operator follows? Data costs a check the core cannot write generally (each service answers differently), plus a declaration key, validation, and `source check` output, for a gain no operator has asked for. Prose costs nothing beyond what SETUP.md already does, and is honest about where the knowledge lives. I would keep prose, and file the data as a bead only with the tracker that needs it.

## 6. The beads it would become

None. If the decision is to close, closing takes no bead: the extraction remainder is already two shaped beads in backlog (yardr-2c8b, then yardr-2y9m blocked by it), custody is with yardr-0qs5 (approved, landing), and yardr-c98n proceeds on its own body with the read-call mechanism that exists. If the decision is to keep the bead for per-action scopes, no build bead can be shaped until that design is written, which is this bead's own work; the keeper's first step is a fresh draft answering open question 2 with a tracker that needs it.
