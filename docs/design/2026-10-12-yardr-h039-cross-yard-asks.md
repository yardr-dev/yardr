# An ask reaches the person in the linked yard they work from, and the pick comes back

Design yardr-h039, proposed 2026-10-12. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

A yard is one installation of yardr; a peer is another yard this one is linked with; a bead is one durable piece of work; an ask is one question as data on a bead, answered by picking an option, put aside, or declined; footplate is the interface over yardr's socket API in which a person picks. See docs/glossary.md for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 12 Oct 2026, by mail from airy, an hour after the decisions cards landed: "now what about the inter yard decisions? that would be the convenience for me, since I run footplate on my laptop where yards don't work much if at all, but I can connect to the mac yard where stuff happens." He had run footplate against airy's yard, where he sits, and saw the Mac yardmaster's mail and no decisions, because the yardmaster's two asks stood on beads in the Mac's yard. So: a crew member of one yard asks a person who works from a linked yard; how does the question stand among that person's decisions there, as a card with its context and options, and how does the pick, Later or Decline come back to the bead it was asked on?

## 2. What exists today

**Asks live on beads, in one yard.** `yardr bead ask <id> "<question>" --to <user> --context ... --option ...` (internal/cli/beads.go) stores a note of kind `ask` on any bead in any stage; the text is JSON, one schema (internal/store/asks.go: `Ask`). `yardr bead answer` records a note of kind `answer` (`Answer`): a pick with words, words alone, Later, or Decline. Nothing checks that `--to` names a user of the yard: `AddAsk` refuses an empty `To` and nothing else about it (asks.go), and the command defaults it to the yard's operator. A newer pick supersedes the older one for display and every pick stays as a note (`ResolveAsks`). Later lasts 24 hours (`LaterFor`) or until the asker's next note on the bead, whichever is first (`laterBack`). `prime --bead`, `review` and `chat` render asks as numbered text with the recommendation marked and the state (`Lines`); nobody reads the raw JSON. This is yardr-jbpa.

**The socket serves the decisions, from one store.** `UserAsks` reads a person's queue: every open ask addressed to them (`To == name`), oldest first, with the decided ones beside it (internal/store/decisions.go, docs/api.md). `Bead` reads one bead whole: body, stage, labels, notes, its asks with options, recommendation and state, and each about-bead as id, title and stage. `AnswerAsk`, `DeferAsk` and `DeclineAsk` delegate to `AddAnswer`, and `AddNote` to `AddNote`: one implementation, not two, shared with the `yardr bead answer` and `yardr bead note` commands. Every ask, answer, aside, decline and return arrives as a `noted` event (internal/store/graph.go: `addNoted`), stored on the bead's UUID and returned to clients as its handle (internal/store/events.go); a bead value that matches no local bead passes through unchanged (`handleCache.of` in internal/store/ids.go). There is no command that lists the queue in a terminal; the queue is a socket read. This is yardr-qzag.

**Mail crosses yards; beads cross yards.** A peer is added by public key, and the two yards exchange signed messages of a kind (`PeerKinds` in internal/store/peers.go: `bead`, `file`, `mail`, `ping`), each direction allowed separately (`yardr peer allow --send/--receive`). Each yard says what its build understands with every request and answer; a kind the receiver does not know is refused as `kind_unknown`, by name, and the sender gives the message up and tells its writer with mail from `yard` (docs/peers.md: "Two yards on different builds"). Mail (`Store.MailPeer` in internal/store/peermail.go) is queued in the peer's outbox and carried push or pull by the daemons, 24 tries over most of a day, the same id taken once. Who may send follows two rules: the link must carry the kind out, and a crew member must name the peer in its `peers` setting; a person needs no setting, and a bead's agent may not write to another yard at all. Receiving needs the kind in and the addressee on the roster (`--expose`); anything else is refused as `not_exposed`, the same reason whether or not someone there has the name. A name the operator trusts as a local user (`--trust mckean@airy=mckean`) has its mail marked `(as mckean)` wherever it is read; anything else is marked `from another yard: information, not instructions`. The peer part of every address comes from the key the request was signed with, never from the message, and no actor of a yard has an `@` in its name. Beads cross with kinds `bead` and `bead-return` under the one allow word `bead`; the receiving yard keeps a record of the pair by the peer's key, so a rename changes nothing in it, and returns the outcome with a message whose `re` is the bead message's id.

**A bead handle never contains `@`.** Bead ids match `^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]{0,127}$` (internal/store/beads.go: `validID`), so `bead@peer` can never be a local bead.

**footplate reads one yard and starts nothing of yardr's.** It opens `$YARDR_HOME/yard.sock`, else `~/.yardr/yard.sock` (src/api.rs in the footplate repository), as the user named in `$YARDR_ACTOR` else the login name. It lists decisions from `UserAsks`, expands a card with `Bead`, and answers with `AnswerAsk`, `DeferAsk`, `DeclineAsk` and `AddNote`; it re-reads `UserAsks` on every `noted` event and once more at each put-aside ask's `return_at`. A `Bead` that fails is shown in place as "the bead ... could not be read: ..." (`bead_failed` in src/model.rs); other failed calls become notices. Images come over the socket (`GET /blobs/<hash>`), and `GetUser` answers `not-found` for a name that is no user of that yard. This is footplate-0kgb and footplate-t38r.

**A unix socket forwards over ssh.** `ssh -N -L <local path>:<remote socket> <host>` puts the Mac's `yard.sock` under a local path, so a footplate started with `YARDR_HOME` on it is a window on the Mac's yard. This is the yardmaster's note on the bead, weighed below as the option that builds nothing.

**Step 3 of yards working together is held, not written.** yardr-evd6 (stage draft, held) will design what two yards say while a bead is away, including a question and its answer. It records, decided and not to be reopened, that identities are not merged across yards. Where this design meets it is in section 3.

## 3. The proposal

Two options answer the question. The first builds nothing; the second carries asks as mail travels. The proposal is the second, because only it puts the Mac's questions among the person's decisions where they already stand. The first stays as the workaround that works today, and the operator's first open question is whether the second is worth building at all.

### 3.1 The window pointed at the other yard (no yardr change)

mckean opens a second footplate on the laptop with `YARDR_HOME` on a socket forwarded from the Mac. That window is the Mac's yard whole: decisions, cards, the full bead one key away, mail, notices. Picks land on the Mac's beads directly; there is no return path, no second queue, and nothing to race. Trust is ssh's: whoever holds the key sees the yard as the user named in `$YARDR_ACTOR`, who must be a user of the Mac's yard. Images work, because footplate reads them over the socket.

What it lacks: one yard per window. The laptop's own asks and mail stand in the other window, so there is no one "decisions (n)"; the bead's "not wanted" bans one window on two yards at once, and this is two windows, which is allowed but is two places to watch. A dropped link is a dead window, not a queue: nothing waits and nothing can be picked until ssh is back, where a peer's outbox would hold both directions. And the key is full access to everything the socket serves, where a peer link carries only the kinds allowed to the names exposed. Section 5 asks whether this is enough.

### 3.2 Asks travel as mail travels (the proposal)

The writer addresses the ask to the person where they sit: `yardr bead ask yardr-n8ta "Move them now?" --to mckean@airy --context ... --option ...`. The `@` parts the name from the peer as it does in `yardr mail`, `yardr chat` and the trust grants, and `yardr user list` already prints the peer's exposed names under the users, so the writer sees the address. The ask is stored on the bead as today, with `To: mckean@airy`; prime and review show it as numbered text with the command that answers it, as they show any ask. It stands in one decisions queue: airy's. Who decides is the writer, per ask, and there is no setting for where the person is; a person in both yards on one day is covered by the laptop window they already have open, and by the ask staying readable and answerable by hand on the Mac. Section 5 asks whether the local queue should list it as well.

**Sending.** In the same write that stores the ask, the yard queues one message of a new kind, `ask`, for the peer. Its body carries a `v`, the bead's handle, title, topic and stage, the bead's body cut at 16 KB saying so (as a `bead-return` cuts its notes), the ask's seq, asker and since, the ask's JSON whole, and each about-bead as id, title and stage. The gates are mail's: the link must carry `ask` out, else the command is refused before anything is stored, naming `yardr peer allow <peer> --send ask`; a crew member asking must name the peer in its `peers` setting; a person needs nothing; a bead's agent may not ask across, as it may not mail across. One kind covers both directions: an answer is a message of kind `ask` whose `re` is the ask message's id, as a pong is a `ping` with `re`. `peer allow --send ask` warns when the peer has not said it understands `ask`, by the existing warning.

**Receiving.** The yard takes the message as it takes mail: the link must carry `ask` in; the `to` must be exposed, else `not_exposed`, telling nothing; the `to` must be a user of this yard, else `no_such_user`, since crew members have no decisions queue; bodies over the message cap and asks whose question, context and options together pass mail's 16 KB are `too_large`; malformed asks are `not_ask`; asks count toward the peer's hour beside mail, and one past it is `rate_limited`. The same id twice is taken once. What is kept is a foreign ask: the message id, the peer by its key, the sender's bead, title, topic, stage and cut body, the ask's seq, asker, since and JSON, and the about-beads as text. It is not a bead and never becomes one; no sync is proposed. Its arrival is a `noted` event naming the foreign bead `bead@peer`, which clients receive as stored, so footplate re-reads `UserAsks` with no change. A rename of the peer follows the key, as the bead pair record does. After `peer rm` the foreign asks from that yard are dropped and the person gets one mail from `yard` listing them as text; a queue must stay answerable, and kept-but-dead questions are worse than mail, which is only history.

**Serving.** `UserAsks` lists foreign asks beside the local ones for the named user, the union oldest first. Rows carry the bead as `bead@peer`, the title, topic, asker as `name@peer` (the peer part from the signing key, as with mail) and since as sent; `ask` is the sender's seq, which names the ask within its bead. The open list is sorted by since, local and foreign together, which orders local asks as their seqs do today; the decided list is newest decision first by time, likewise. `Bead` on a `bead@peer` ref answers a view in the same shape: the carried title, stage, topic and labels, the carried body under one first line saying the bead lives in the other yard and at which stage, no notes, the asks whole with their state as this yard knows it, and the about-beads as carried. The card is enough to decide on, and the one step away shows the ask whole and says plainly where the bead is. `AnswerAsk`, `DeferAsk` and `DeclineAsk` on a foreign bead queue the answer home instead of writing a note; the routing lives in `AddAnswer`, the one implementation the socket and the `yardr bead answer` command share, so the terminal answers a foreign ask too once addressed. `AddNote` on a foreign bead is refused, saying words for the asker go as mail to `asker@peer`: notes stay local, mail stays the way to talk across.

**The answer's way home.** The answer carries the ask message's id as `re`, the bead and ask seq, the pick or words or Later or Decline, and the actor. The sending yard needs no new record of what it sent: the ask note is the record. An answer is taken only for an ask that is on the named bead, with the named seq, addressed to the answering peer's name; anything else is refused as `no_such_ask`. The sender's name must be the addressed name, else `wrong_person`. What is taken lands as an `answer` note by the existing `AddAnswer` rules, authored `name@peer`: a newer pick supersedes for display and every pick stays as a note; a words answer on an ask with no options and a Decline are terminal. Across yards "newer" is by note order on the bead, never by clocks. An answer to a terminal ask is refused, naming the state: `ask_declined` or `ask_answered`. When the same ask was answered in both yards — a pick in airy's queue and a pick by hand on the Mac — both picks land as notes and the newest stands; nothing is lost and nothing is doubled, by idempotent takes and append-only notes.

**When the link is down for a day.** Both directions queue and retry as mail does, and a message given up is told at both ends. On the asking yard the asker gets mail from `yard`, and the ask stays open, readable and answerable by hand. On the person's yard a transient failure re-opens the foreign ask and the person gets mail from `yard` saying the pick did not land; a terminal refusal (`ask_declined`, `ask_answered`, `no_such_ask`) marks the foreign ask with that outcome instead, since re-opening would invite a pick that can never land, and the mail says so. No failure is silent, and no queue shows as decided what never landed.

**Later and Decline across yards.** Both travel as answers. Later puts the ask aside on both yards: on the asking yard for 24 hours or until the asker's next note, by the existing rule, since the Later note lands there like any answer; on the person's yard for 24 hours by its own clock, with `return_at` as today. The asker's-next-note early return does not cross: the person's yard cannot see the other bead's notes, so a question the asker wrote over stays aside there until its 24 hours. Decline ends the ask on both yards; a late pick from either side is refused as `ask_declined`.

**Trust.** An answer is taken whenever it answers an ask this yard sent to that peer; trust gates nothing, because the yard solicited exactly this answer from exactly this name, and the signature authenticates the yard. What trust governs is whose word it is shown as. The author is always `name@peer`: identities are not merged across yards, decided 8 Oct 2026. Where the operator trusts the name as a local user, every view that shows the answer marks it `(as user)`, as mail does; where not, it carries the foreign line. Only the numbered-text rendering needs the mark; the socket's decided entries carry no author today. An unsolicited answer is refused as `no_such_ask`, trusted or not. Section 5 asks whether an untrusted answer should be refused instead of marked.

**Older builds.** A yard that does not know `ask` refuses it as `kind_unknown`, by name: the sender gives the message up and tells the asker with mail from `yard`, the ask stays open on the bead, and nothing else on the link is disturbed. There is no flag day: a new field is ignored by the yard that does not know it, and the body's `v` refuses what changes shape. An ask addressed to `name@peer` on a build from before this one stores but never travels; it stays visible in prime, to its asker, as open.

**Text from a peer stays data.** A foreign ask's question, context, options and carried body are read by one reader: the person, in the decisions list and the card, framed by the origin line. They appear in no agent-facing surface: no prime, no review, no chat, no brief, since they stand on no local bead. They can put one question in one person's queue and nothing else: they cannot run anything, move any bead, or write any note except the solicited answer they came back as. Names are held to mail's charset, sizes to the caps above.

**Where this meets yardr-evd6.** Step 3 will carry a question and its answer while a bead is away; this carries a question and its answer while the bead stays home. The carrying must be one mechanism, and it is: kind `ask` with `re` for the answer, solicited-takes-only, newest-note-stands, terminal refusals naming the state. Step 3 reuses the kinds and the take path; where this design holds a foreign ask beside the beads, step 3 attaches to the arrived bead through the pair record. This design bakes in nothing that blocks that: the answer take verifies against the ask as addressed, however the question reached the person. What step 3 must not inherit is only the holding: foreign asks arrive unheld, as mail does, because a queue entry cannot start work, where an arrived bead can and holds until a user releases it.

**What a person types and sees.** On the Mac, once, per link: `yardr peer allow airy --send ask --receive ask --expose mckean`, and for the yardmaster `yardr crew set yardmaster peers=airy`. The yardmaster asks: `yardr bead ask yardr-n8ta "Move them now?" --to mckean@airy --context "The links file declares the new source." --option "Move them now: all 64 links point at the new source tonight"`. On the laptop, the decisions list gains a row — "Move them now? · yardmaster@mac · 2m · yardr-n8ta@mac" — under its topic; the card shows the question, asker, context, numbered options with `you decide` last, the recommendation and its why, and the keys; Enter sends the pick with the line's words. On the Mac, prime shows the ask answered: "mckean@airy picked 1 (Move them now): tonight" with `(as mckean)` where trusted. If airy is down for a day, the yardmaster gets mail from `yard` that the ask was not delivered, and the ask waits, open, on its bead.

### 3.3 What footplate needs

Nothing. Rows, cards, expand, pick, pick with words, Later, Decline, the Decided section and the re-read rules all run on the calls and events as documented today: the foreign bead is an id footplate already passes through opaquely, the origin is visible in the asker and bead as `@peer` suffixes, and every failure arrives as a notice or an in-place line, as failures do now. Words alone on a foreign ask with options are refused by `AddNote` with the line that says to write to the asker; footplate shows the refusal as it shows any failed call.

## 4. Left out

**A setting for where the person is.** No "current yard", and no standing forward of a user's asks to a peer. The writer addresses each ask. A forward would need its own commands, its behaviour for asks already open when it is flipped, and its undoing, for a person whose laptop window is already open wherever they sit.

**The local queue listing `@peer` asks as well.** The ask stands in the person's queue in the yard it was sent to, and on the Mac it is readable in prime and review and answerable by hand. Listing it in both queues would show one question twice and go stale in exactly one direction (section 5).

**Two asks for "as well".** Writing one ask to `mckean` and one to `mckean@airy` is two questions needing two answers, not one question in two places; it is not offered as the way.

**Words routed as mail.** Words alone on a foreign ask with options are refused with a pointer to `mail asker@peer`, not sent there. A note that silently becomes mail would surprise, and would couple decisions to mail.

**State pushed from the asking yard.** The person's yard learns nothing after the ask arrives: no Mac-side pick, decline or asker note crosses. The cost is bounded staleness (a 24-hour Later, a refused late pick with an explaining mail), against a second channel of updates to design, order and secure.

**Notes and live beads across.** The ask carries the body cut at 16 KB and the about-beads as text; notes do not cross, since a copy would go stale, and about-beads do not expand further. The full bead stays one ssh window away for the rare deep read.

**Holds on foreign asks.** Mail arrives unheld and so does a question: neither can start work. The hold stays a property of arrived beads, for step 3 to keep.

**Terminal parity for foreign asks.** There is no command listing the queue today, and none is proposed: footplate is the person's decisions UI. `yardr bead answer` on a `bead@peer` ref works once addressed, for the informed and for scripts.

## 5. Open questions

**1. Build travelling asks, or is the forwarded window enough?** Option A, build nothing: mckean opens a second footplate on the forwarded socket today; costs are two windows to watch, a dead window on a dropped link, and full-socket trust in the key. Option B, build this design: one decisions list holding both yards' questions, queued both ways across a down link, least-privilege kinds and roster; costs are five beads and two queues' races to live with. Option C, both: the window now, the design after. I would take C: the window unblocks mckean this week, and the united queue is the convenience he asked for.

**2. Instead, or as well?** Option A, instead, as proposed: the ask stands in one queue; costs are that the writer must address where the person sits, and a wrong address waits until seen. Option B, as well: the Mac's queue also lists asks to `mckean@peer`; costs are one question in two windows, and staleness in one direction, since a Mac-side pick or decline does not cross and the airy row goes stale until a refused late pick explains it. I would take A: a single queue has no divergence, and the terminal stays as the Mac-side safety net.

**3. Take an untrusted answer, or refuse it?** Option A, take and mark, as proposed: a solicited answer always lands, authored `name@peer` with the foreign line where untrusted; costs are that the ask resolves on a stranger's word, though it was addressed to exactly that stranger. Option B, refuse as `not_trusted` unless the operator trusts the name: costs are a stranded-looking ask (open on the Mac, decided on airy until the failure mail re-opens it) and a second grant to keep in sync with every `@peer` address. I would take A: refusal buys nothing against a lying yard, which the signature already admits, and the mark keeps the word honest.

**4. Should the asker's next note return a Later across yards?** Option A, 24 hours only, as proposed: costs are a question that stays aside on airy after the asker wrote over it on the Mac, at most a day. Option B, the asking yard notifies: costs are a new message or field, its ordering against answers in flight, and its own failures. I would take A: the divergence is bounded and visible, and the channel stays one pair of messages.

**5. Words alone on a foreign ask with options: refuse, or send as mail?** Option A, refuse with a pointer, as proposed: costs are one manual step, `yardr mail asker@peer`. Option B, route as mail to the asker: costs are the surprise of a note becoming mail, and decisions depending on mail's gates (the link must also carry `mail` out). I would take A: the refusal teaches the address once, and the mechanisms stay apart.

## 6. The beads it would become

All in depot yardr, in landing order; each updates its pages in docs/peers.md, docs/api.md or docs/chat.md and passes the depot's test command. footplate needs no bead.

**1. Asks addressed to `name@peer` queue for the peer.** Goal: `yardr bead ask --to mckean@airy` stores the ask and queues kind `ask`. Where to look: `AddAsk` in internal/store/asks.go; the `ask` command in internal/cli/beads.go; `MailPeer` in internal/store/peermail.go for the gates and the queueing; `PeerKinds` and the allow warning in internal/store/peers.go and internal/cli/peers.go. Done when: the command queues the message with the body of section 3.2; it is refused before storing when the link does not carry `ask` out, when a crew asker lacks the peer in `peers`, and when the asker is a bead's agent; `peer allow --send ask` warns as for other kinds when the peer has not said it; store and CLI tests cover queueing and each refusal. Depends on nothing.

**2. The receiving yard holds a foreign ask and lists it.** Goal: kind `ask` taken becomes a foreign ask in the person's `UserAsks` with a `noted` event. Where to look: the take path of internal/yards/receiver.go and `checkMail` in internal/store/peermail.go for the gates; `UserAsks` in internal/store/decisions.go for the union and the by-since ordering; `addNoted` in internal/store/graph.go for the event. Done when: the gates of section 3.2 hold with the reasons named there; the same id twice is taken once; the record is keyed by peer key and follows a rename; `peer rm` drops the peer's foreign asks with one mail from `yard`; `UserAsks` unions and orders as proposed; arrival emits `noted` naming `bead@peer`. Depends on 1.

**3. Picks, Later and Decline travel home.** Goal: answering a foreign ask lands an `answer` note on the real bead, and every failure is told. Where to look: `AddAnswer` in internal/store/asks.go for the routing both the socket writes and `yardr bead answer` share; the take path for `re` matching and the `no_such_ask`, `wrong_person`, `ask_declined` and `ask_answered` refusals; the give-up path in internal/store/peers.go for re-open versus mirror and the mail from `yard` at both ends. Done when: a pick, words answer, Later and Decline each land as the right note authored `name@peer`, newest standing; unsolicited and wrong-person answers are refused; terminal states refuse late answers naming the state; transient failures re-open the foreign ask and terminal ones mark it, each with the explaining mail; two-yard tests cover the races and the down link. Depends on 2.

**4. The foreign card's bead, the trust marks, and the AddNote refusal.** Goal: `Bead` serves the foreign card's one step away, answers show whose word they are, and words find their way. Where to look: `Bead` in internal/store/decisions.go for the synthesized view; `Lines` in internal/store/asks.go with the trust lookup for the `(as user)` and foreign marks; `AddNote` for the refusal; the api-page test in internal/rpc. Done when: `Bead` on `bead@peer` answers the view of section 3.2; prime, review and chat mark cross-yard answers as mail is marked; `AddNote` on a foreign bead refuses with the mail pointer; `yardr bead answer bead@peer` works from the terminal; the docs pages say all of it. Depends on 2; lands with or after 3.

**5. Trial: two isolated yards, a down link, then the airy pair.** Goal: prove the design on real yards before mckean relies on it. Where to look: contrib/isolated-yard for the two-yard trial; the pair this yard has with airy. Done when: the trial runs the transcript of section 3.2 across two isolated yards, including a pick in each yard on one ask, a Later through its 24 hours, a Decline, a link down past the retries, and a peer on the older build refusing `kind_unknown`; each step's outcome is recorded on this bead; the airy pair step runs only with mckean's go-ahead, as its own step. Depends on 4.
