# The web view's later steps: what is left after observe, act and the edge were decided elsewhere

Design yardr-nxah, proposed 2026-10-12. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

A yard is one installation of yardr: its store, its crew and its depots. A depot is a git repository or a plain directory that beads are worked in; a bead is one durable piece of work; a flow is the stages a bead goes through and a stage is one step of it; a group is who works a stage; the operator is the person who runs the yard. A pack is a way of working as one directory: flows, groups, roles, crew members; applying one to a depot is an import. The web view is `yardr web serve`, the browser view of the yard (docs/web.md). An ask is one question as data on a bead, answered by picking an option. docs/glossary.md has all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 4 Oct 2026: a web view to watch beads travel through flows, in real time, and possibly also the configuration of flows, rigs and residents; a pack is a start, not a cage. mckean, 11 Oct 2026: "the web view is a bit cluttered and not great as an overview. also there's like a multi scroll pane view, single scroll is the way. so perhaps first a cleanup/simplification to make it easier to read." This bead was filed when none of that existed and ordered the work observe first, then act, then configure. It is now older than much of what it speaks of: step by step, other beads built or decided nearly all of it. So the question this document answers is the one the yardmaster put with the bead when it was sent to draft on 12 Oct 2026: what of this bead is already built, what is already decided by another design, and what is left, if anything, to design.

## 2. What exists today

### 2.1. Already built: observe, the cleanup, and the phone's first screen

Step 1, the read-only live view, is built: train yardr-e3rg with children yardr-tzzc, yardr-ardv, yardr-822r and yardr-68k3 is merged. `yardr web serve` (docs/web.md) shows the home feed, flows, beads, one bead, sessions, crew and what waits for a person, live over one server-sent-events stream, every page also answering JSON. It is server-rendered HTML with vendored htmx, no Node and no build step; every route in `internal/web/server.go` names GET, "so the mux refuses every other method itself: the view changes nothing". The JSON routes and the stream are a second front end's API: signalbox reads the yard only through them, served by `yardr web serve --ui <dir>` (design yardr-zyzp, merged; its build beads yardr-fkkt and signalbox-6cyw closed).

The cleanup mckean asked for on 11 Oct 2026 is built: yardr-1707 ("one page scroll, and a home page that leads with what needs a person") is merged. And the direction for observing on a phone is decided and built: design yardr-8y0f ("the web view's home on a phone answers 'does anything need me?' on the first screen, and nothing else") is merged, with all three children merged (yardr-mhm8 and yardr-k8z9, the yard panel; yardr-m2r7, home answers first).

### 2.2. Already decided by another design: act, and who the view acts as

Acting from the browser is decided by design yardr-8wxe ("yardr as a service"), decided on 12 Oct 2026 with the draft's pick on every open question (mckean's mail of that day, event 17313: "So every open question takes the draft's pick"). Its proposal, section 3.4 item 2:

> "The view is started for a user (`yardr web serve --as <user>`), and then takes the writes that user may make, each an existing socket call: mail to a crew member and the conversation read back (`Mail`, `Conversation`), then the picks on an ask (`AnswerAsk`, `DeferAsk`, `DeclineAsk`) and the outcome of a bead that waits for a person (what `yardr review` does), each sent with the bead's revision. Without `--as` the view is as today: GET only."

The same item says plainly whose step this was: "This is step 1 of yardr-nxah cut into two slices". Open question 6 asked whether the act step is built from that design, and the decided answer is yes, after the trial: "the act step is built from this design after the trial". The two slices exist as build beads in backlog: yardr-y6ty ("The web view, started for a user, sends mail to a crew member and shows the conversation", blocked by the trial yardr-aj24) and yardr-bvt3 ("The web view answers asks and sends on a bead that waits for a person", blocked by yardr-y6ty). Open question 4 decided who the view acts as: one user named at start (`--as`); the other option, "a header the proxy sets, mapped to a user", is left as "the step for a team".

This bead's open question about authentication for browser actions is answered by the same decision together with mckean's own line of 11 Oct 2026: security is outside yardr for now (a private network, Kratos and Oathkeeper in front), so the act step does not wait for a login built into yardr. Design yardr-8wxe states the arrangement: "the service's edge (Kratos and Oathkeeper) is what says which logged-in account reaches which yard's view". Design yardr-xpf8 (merged), which decided how a footplate off the yard's machine proves who is calling, confirms the web view needs nothing more: "The web view of design yardr-8wxe is the same statement in its simplest form: user X, said by whoever started the view, for every request alike. Nothing here changes it." Its table of shapes names the view as "the mobile footplate that needs no client" and "the first thing a phone shows". This matches the yard's standing memories: networking is "define what to allow", never a proxy built into yardr; no login server of yardr's own.

Two neighbouring decisions close off the rest of the reaching-the-view question. Whether `--host` may name a loopback view without `--unsafe`, and whether `--unsafe` stays, are open questions on design bead yardr-7mkc, still in draft; reaching the view through a proxy is the operator's setup outside yardr per the 9 Oct 2026 decision, not yardr work. Design yardr-r6jh (merged) sets the rule for acting across yards: "Nothing that acts follows ... A person who wants to act in the other yard acts there: in its terminal, through its socket forwarded over ssh, or through its web view at an edge."

### 2.3. What is left: read-only config pages, and the customised state

Two items of this bead have no design and no bead.

**The read-only config pages.** The JSON lists exist and the pages do not. docs/web.md, "The lists": "`/depots`, `/groups`, `/routes`, `/peers`, `/deps` and `/limits` have no page: they answer JSON whatever the request accepts." Each answers what a list command prints with `--json` (`yardr depot list`, `yardr group list`, `yardr route list`, `yardr peer list`, every bead's `yardr dep list`, `yardr limits`), under a key named as the route, beside the yard's newest sequence number. Their handlers live in `internal/web/lists.go`, their routes in `internal/web/server.go`, and docs/web.md, "Adding a page", gives the pattern a new page follows: one data struct with `json` tags, one template defining `content`, one handler. Design yardr-8wxe names these pages as staying with this bead: if the act step is built from that design, "yardr-nxah keeps its later steps (configuration pages, the customised state)".

**The toggles and the customised state.** Per item, the rig's repository config or the yard's local one. The switch exists per role and per flow at the command line: `yardr depot source <depot> role <group> depot|pack` and `yardr depot source <depot> flow depot|pack` (docs/configuration.md; `yardr depot sources <depot>` shows the whole table). The third state does not exist: a yard-local copy of a pack's installed file, marked customised, that `pack apply` keeps and names. What exists instead is a refusal: where the installed copy was edited by hand since the last import, `pack apply` overwrites it only with `--yes` (docs/packs.md: "the edit belongs in the pack's source ... ; --yes overwrites it"). No bead covers the customised state: no open or closed bead names it. This bead's own ordering stands: that state comes before any editing in a browser, and the web view never writes configuration at repository level (mckean, 4 Oct 2026).

## 3. The proposal

There is no web-view design left to do here, and this document proposes none. The observe step is built, the act step is decided with build beads filed, and authentication for browser actions is decided as outside yardr. What is left is small, and each piece already has its mechanism:

1. **Read-only config pages as straight build work.** Six pages (`/depots`, `/groups`, `/routes`, `/peers`, `/deps`, `/limits`) on the "Adding a page" pattern, each rendering the JSON value its route already answers. Each is live as the other list pages are: its handler reads the head before the data and passes `stream: "seq"`, and its template re-reads the page on every event of the shell's one stream (`hx-trigger="sse:message"`), as `/beads` does. It is `seq` because these changes log no event of their own — no `LogEvent` call in `internal/store/routing.go`, `graph.go` or `peers.go` — so the page refreshes on the yard's other events, and `seq` sends a message for every one. Each is reached from the menu, which gains one entry per page; nothing else is new. No new mechanism: the data structs, handlers and routes exist; only the templates, the page half of the handlers and the six menu rows are new. A person opening `/routes` would see the yard's routes in match order, each naming its stage, group and runner, as `yardr route list` prints them; the other five likewise. Whether they are built now or left JSON-only until a person misses them is open question 1.
2. **The customised state as its own design bead, not part of this one.** It is pack behaviour, not web work: how an installed copy is marked customised, where the mark lives, what `pack apply` keeps and names, and how `yardr depot sources` shows it. Whether that bead is drafted now or parked until someone wants to edit in a browser is open question 3.
3. **Close yardr-nxah when this design lands.** The bead's steps are then each somewhere: observe built, act filed, configure proposed below. A short document that ends a bead is a good result. Whether mckean agrees is open question 2.

## 4. Left out

- **Any editing or toggling in the browser.** The bead's own ordering forbids it before the customised state exists, and no browser editing is proposed here. Read-only pages show state; `yardr depot source` remains the switch.
- **Per-request authentication in the view** (resolving the browser's session per request, several people acting as themselves behind one edge). Design yardr-8wxe left this as "the step for a team" and design yardr-xpf8 declined it in its section 4. One user per view, named at start, stands.
- **Reaching the view through a proxy, and the `--unsafe` policy.** The proxy is the operator's setup outside yardr (9 Oct 2026 decision); the allow questions live on yardr-7mkc.
- **Push of the yard's events to a phone, and a native phone client.** Telling a person outside the yard is the `notify` source on their user record (the Pushover or Telegram pack); design yardr-xpf8 left a native phone footplate for after its trial. The browser view is the phone's screen.
- **Creating beads from the browser.** Design yardr-8wxe considered and declined it: "With mail to the yardmaster the customer has everything else the yard does, since the yardmaster creates, shapes and advances beads when asked; no page for creating a bead is proposed."

## 5. Open questions

1. **Are the six read-only config pages built now, or do the lists stay JSON-only until a person misses them?** Building now costs two small build beads (section 6, B1 and B2) and six pages to keep as the lists grow; a person browsing the yard gets the whole configuration readable beside the beads. Leaving them costs nothing now; signalbox and other JSON clients already have everything, and the rule in docs/web.md ("A new list command gets its route with it, and an old one when a client needs it") already says routes appear on need. I would build them now: the data is answered already, each page is one template on a documented pattern, and a yard whose web view cannot show its own depots, groups and routes is not finished as an overview.
2. **Does yardr-nxah close when this design lands?** Closing says the accounting in section 2 is right and the leftovers travel as the beads below, owned by the yardmaster like any other. Keeping it open says this bead should stay the home of the later steps until they are built, and the proposed beads would then be its children or links instead. I would close it: every step is built, decided with beads filed, or proposed below, and a stale umbrella that stays open collects stale questions.
3. **Is the customised state drafted now, or parked until someone wants to edit in a browser?** Drafting now costs one design round on pack behaviour with no builder waiting for it; parking costs nothing, and the refusal rule (`--yes` overwrites a hand-edited installed copy) already protects the edit. The bead's old question, whether "customised" is per file or also per object, would open that draft either way. I would park it: nothing proposed here or in design yardr-8wxe needs it, and a design drafted against a real editing proposal will be better than one drafted against this bead's ordering alone.

## 6. The beads it would become

For depot yardr, in the order they would land. B1 and B2 wait for open question 1; B3 waits for open question 3. None waits for the act slices (yardr-y6ty, yardr-bvt3): read-only pages need no user and no edge.

**B1. Read-only pages for the depot, group and route lists.** Goal: `/depots`, `/groups` and `/routes` answer HTML to a browser as today they answer JSON, each page rendering the value its route already serves, live: the handler reads the head before the data and passes `stream: "seq"`, and the template wraps what changes in an element that re-reads the page on every event of the shell's one stream, as `/beads` does; the JSON of each route is unchanged, field for field. The menu gains one entry per page, each page's `Menu` naming its own entry. Where to look: `internal/web/lists.go` (the handlers, ending in `listed`), `internal/web/server.go` (`routes`, `menu`, `Page.stream`), `internal/web/templates/beads.html` (the re-reading element), docs/web.md ("The lists", "Adding a page", "Live pages", and the content security policy: no inline script, no other host), `internal/web/assets/README.md` for the vendored files a page may use. Done when: the three pages render the same rows as `yardr depot list`, `yardr group list` and `yardr route list --json`; a test asserts each page's HTML carries the live re-read (`hx-get` of itself with `hx-trigger="sse:message"`) and the shell's stream in `seq` format, extending the per-page maps the `/beads` and column tests keep (`internal/web/beads_test.go`, `TestColumnIsLive` in `internal/web/line_test.go`); the menu links to all three and `TestNoOtherHost`'s walk reaches them (extend its must-reach list), which also fails on a URL naming another host; docs/web.md lists the new pages (the Pages row and the live-pages sentence); `sh .yardr/test` passes.

**B2. Read-only pages for the peer, edge and quota lists.** Goal: the same as B1 for `/peers`, `/deps` and `/limits`: live pages rendering the values already answered, each reached from the menu, JSON unchanged. For `/limits` the re-read costs no extra provider run: the route asks each source at most once a minute per kind however often it is read, and the page reads through the same handler. Where to look: as B1, plus docs/peers.md for what a peer row means, `internal/web/limits.go` (`handleLimits`, the per-kind throttle) and docs/web.md on `/limits`. Done when: the three pages render the same rows as `yardr peer list --json`, the yard's edges, and `yardr limits`; the liveness assertions, menu links, walk reachability and docs/web.md updated as in B1; `sh .yardr/test` passes. Depends on nothing but the pattern B1 sets; it lands after B1 so the second slice follows the first.

**B3. Design: a yard-local copy of a pack's installed file, marked customised, that `pack apply` keeps and names.** Goal: a design bead in the design flow that decides the third state this bead always assumed: how the mark is stored, per file or also per object (this bead's old open question, carried over); what `pack apply` does with a marked copy on import, with and without `--yes`, and how it names what it kept; how `yardr depot sources` and a later read-only page show the state; what `pack apply --prune` does with it. Where to look: `internal/pack/plan.go` (what an import overwrites), docs/packs.md ("`edited by hand since the last import`", `yardr pack apply`), docs/configuration.md (`yardr depot source`, `yardr depot sources`), this document's section 2.3. Done when: the design is decided and its build beads are filed. Depends on nothing; anything that edits configuration in a browser depends on it.
