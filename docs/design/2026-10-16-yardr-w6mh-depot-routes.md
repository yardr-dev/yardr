# Routes belong to depots, with yard defaults every depot inherits

Design yardr-w6mh, proposed 2026-10-16. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

A yard is one installation of yardr: its store, its crew and its depots. A depot is a git repository or a plain directory that beads are worked in; a bead is one durable piece of work. A flow is the stages a bead passes through and the moves allowed between them; a stage is one step of a flow. A route says which group takes the beads of a stage, and a group is who works it: a pool of agents, a script, people, or another yard. A runner is how a group's work starts (`herdr`, `exec`, `manual`, `peer`). The dispatcher is the part of the daemon that starts sessions. A pack is a way of working as one directory: flows, groups, roles, crew members. The operator is the person who runs the yard; a user is a person of the yard; the originator of a bead is the person the bead is for, whose subscription pays for building it. A yard default (new in this design) is a route that belongs to the yard and serves every depot that does not state its own route for the same beads; a depot's own route belongs to that depot alone. See docs/glossary.md for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, with the yardmaster on 16 Oct 2026, after `yardr route list` had grown to 108 routes — most of them one rule copied once per depot, ties between matching routes ordered invisibly, priority numbers whose meaning lived in memory, routes with no note and no way to edit one except remove and re-add, and nothing that said beforehand where a bead would go — asked about routing across depots, then settled it: “it would still belong in a depot”. This design makes that exact: every route belongs to a depot or is a yard default, a depot's own route overrides a default only for the beads it states, and the table becomes small enough to read, hard to tie by accident, and answerable before a bead moves.

## 2. What exists today

**The route record and the match.** A route is `Route` in `internal/store/routing.go` (lines 407–427): a stage, optional filters for one type (`Type`), one depot (`Depot`), one or more labels (`Label` and `AndLabels`, read through `Labels()`), a bead priority (`BeadPriority`), an originator (`Originator`), the first group (`Group`), an integer `Priority`, and a quota chain (`When` on the first group and `Else`, read through `Groups()` and chosen by `Choose`). There is no note, no name, and no scope beside the depot filter: a route with no `--depot` has `Depot` nil and matches beads in every depot. `MatchRoute` (same file) returns the first route in `SortRoutes` order that matches the bead's stage, type, depot, labels, bead priority and originator. `SortRoutes` orders by higher priority first, then by specificity — how many filters are set, each label counting, a named originator counting and `any` counting as none — then by route id, so two routes that match the same bead at the same priority and specificity are ordered by an id nobody chose. `yardr route list` held 108 routes on 16 Oct 2026 (109 lines with the header; counted again while drafting and still 108): 102 naming a depot and 6 with no depot. The depots are aiquokka (9), footplate (17), signalbox (22), yardr (21), yardr-design (13), yardr-hosted (10) and yardr.dev (10). By stage: `new` 28, `review` 14, `decide` 13, `approved` 11, `backlog` 11, and the rest in ones and twos. The copies the bead names are there: `decide` to the manual group `decisions` 13 times, `backlog` to `backlog` 11 times, plain `review` to `yardr-reviewers` in every depot, `review` with the label `brakeman-review` to `brakeman-reviews` three times at priority 10.

**The yardr duplicate.** Depot yardr has two plain `new` routes at priority 0 to the same first group with the same fallback (`57h28dx9dx9cmydnrzj9` with the condition `Weekly<target+10`, and `y7qe3ems183zfz31md09` with `Weekly<target`, each `--else muse-builders`). They match exactly the same beads at the same priority and specificity, so the first in id order always wins and the second never routes anything; nothing said so when the second was added. This is the silent tie in its smallest form: same filters, same rank, different quota threshold.

**Writing routes.** `yardr route add`, `route list` and `route rm` are one command group (`routeCmd` in `internal/cli/ops.go`). `AddRoute` in the store checks that every named group exists, that a quota chain names only groups that start agents (or one `exec` group gated on a source), that a named depot and a named originator exist, and then mints a fresh id (`newEdgeID`); it does not check whether the new route ties or duplicates one already there. A route cannot be edited: there is no `route set`, so a change is `route rm` plus `route add`, with a new id and, between the two, either no route or two. `route list` prints the stored routes in match order with the columns ID, STAGE, TYPE, DEPOT, LABEL, BEADS, ORIGINATOR, PRI and GROUP; it has no `--depot` flag (`yardr route list --depot yardr` answers `unknown flag: --depot`), so it shows every depot's routes ranked together even though routes of different depots never compete for one bead — a bead is in one depot, and only that depot's routes plus the depot-less ones can match it. Priority therefore reads as a yard-wide scale in the list and is only ever a per-bead contest in fact. `DeleteRoute` removes by id; beads already claimed through the route keep its group until their sessions end, which `route rm` reports.

**Who may write.** Since yardr-fz1j (landed 16 Oct), adding a route is attribution-only: anyone may call `AddRoute`, and the write is recorded as the actor resolved from the caller's pane, so a session cannot pass as someone else. Removing a route is the operator's alone (`operatorOnly` with the `Alone` mark in `DeleteRoute`: “only the operator may remove a route”). Bead yardr-g7kq, open and waiting to be built, narrows that one line: the crew member named by the `yardmaster` setting may also remove a route, because the yardmaster adds the replacement today and cannot remove the original, which is how duplicates accumulate. No other write changes in that bead.

**Where a route is chosen, and what already says why.** The dispatcher (`route` in `internal/dispatch/dispatch.go`) lists dispatchable beads, sorts the routes once, takes `Match` (the dispatch spelling of `MatchRoute`) for each bead, then `chooseGroup` (`internal/dispatch/quota.go`) picks the first group of the route whose quota condition holds, asking the yard's limits source only for a conditioned route and at most once in five minutes per provider. A bead no condition holds for waits with one `quota_wait` event naming the route, each group passed over with its reason in words, and the earliest reset. A claimed bead's `claimed` event names the `route` and any quota `passed` groups. Two read-only views already reuse that record without asking a provider: `yardr bead show` prints one line for a claimed or waiting bead (“routed to … by route …: … passed over (…)”, from `routingOf` in `internal/cli/routewhy.go`), and the group-wait view (`waitFor` in the same file) recomputes the match to say which group a ready bead waits for and by which route. What no view does is answer for a bead that has not been claimed: which routes were considered, which one wins, which were passed over in the match (as opposed to passed over by quota), and which defaults a depot's own routes set aside. Other readers of the match include who works a stage for a bead not yet there (`stageWork` in `internal/store/flowmap.go`, behind `yardr flow show`, the brief's outcomes and the web view's flow columns), the group a bead answers to before any claim (`stageGroup` in `internal/store/routing.go`, behind who may move a bead out of a stage of people), arrivals from linked yards, the bead ETA's stage cells, and the web board. Each calls `MatchRoute` on the whole sorted table for a bead that names its depot, so they all give one answer today and must keep giving one answer.

**Flows, the pattern for “the depot's own or the yard's”.** A flow is stored once under a name, or read from a depot's repository under `.yardr/flows/`; which one governs a bead is decided by `resolveFlow` (`internal/store/flows.go`): a pin on the bead, then the store pointer, then the depot's repository flows, then none — and within the store and within the repository the type's pointer or file wins over the depot-wide one (`FlowPointer`, set by `SetFlowPointer`, per depot and optionally per type). `yardr depot list` shows the result as `default (repo@c8fb11f)` or `starter.default (store)`: one line says whose flow each depot follows. Routes have no equivalent: the depot filter is a match filter, not an ownership with a fallback, and a depot-less route outranks a depot's own route whenever its priority is higher. That is visible in design drafts today: the depot-less `draft` route for type `design` (priority 3, to `muse-wonder`) outranks the `draft` routes depots yardr and footplate state for themselves (priority 0, to `muse-builders`), so those two own routes never win. They are dead, and the table does not say so.

**Packs and depot setup write per-depot copies.** A pack describes a depot's line without naming the yard: each stage with a group becomes one route for (depot, stage, and the type for a type's flow), written by the planner (`routes` in `internal/pack/plan_flows.go`) and applied add-then-delete (`internal/pack/apply.go`), because `route add` mints a new id per call (docs/packs.md, “Routes”). The planner recognises the routes that name the depot and ask nothing of the single bead (`ByBead` in the store: no label, no bead priority, no named originator) as the stage's routes — a quota route among them counts by its first group and is replaced with the rest when that group differs — while routes by label or priority and depot-less routes are left alone and listed as not in the pack. Export walks stricter ground in reverse (`routes` in `internal/pack/export.go`): only a plain depot route that wins its stage is followed, and a stage worked by a depot-less (there called yard-wide) route, or by one with a condition or further groups, is written with no group and listed as left out, because no pack carries such a route. `yardr depot init <depot>` is a pack import plus a guessed test command, so a new depot today gets its own copy of every stage route, including the stages every depot shares (`backlog` to `backlog`, `decide` to `decisions` in the starter pack). Routes stay in the yard's store and never in the repository: no file in a depot names a route, and this design keeps it so, for the reason settled in the question — a route says who does the work on this yard's groups and quota, which a repository does not know.

**Quota chains, originators and labels, as they stand.** A route's quota chain is settled machinery (design yardr-xf5c, now built): `--group 'a@Weekly<80' --else b --else wait`, the condition holds or the next group is tried, a condition that cannot be evaluated holds, a group at its limit is waited for rather than passed over, and packs bring no condition. Originators are settled too (design yardr-2k0x, docs/groups.md): a route without `--originator` takes only the operator's beads, a named one only that user's, `any` every originator's, and a route whose first group starts no agents (people, a script, another yard) is stored as `any` by `AddRoute`, since it burns no local subscription; a named originator counts as one filter in specificity, `any` and none as none. Cross-depot label routes exist in three shapes today: estimate labels that are already depot-less (`difficulty:3` in `new` to `muse-wonder` at priority 3, `difficulty:1` and `difficulty:2` to `muse-builders` at priority 1, and `difficulty:3` in `review-security` at priority 5); harness labels copied per depot to different pools (`muse` to `muse-light` in three depots, to `muse-builders` in two, to `muse-hosted` in one; `claude` to `yardr-builders` in three, counting yardr-hosted's route that spells the operator's name out); and the `brakeman-review` label above. Stage names are per flow and collide across flows: `review` is a stage of the starter `default` flow, of signalbox's `contributors.pr` flow (whose pull-request beads have their own route by `--type pr` to `signalbox-pr-reviewers`), of aiquokka's `pr-review` flow (by `--type pr-review`), and yardr-design's train flow (by `--type train`) — all to different groups, kept apart today only by depot and type specificity, since a route names no flow and `MatchRoute` never reads the bead's flow: a bead carries at most a pin (`flow=<name>` in its metadata, `PinnedFlow` in `internal/store/flows.go`), and its governing flow is resolved from pin, depot and type when a reader needs the flow itself, which routing never does.

## 3. The proposal

The smallest change that answers the question is to give the depot filter the second job it almost has — ownership with fallback — and to keep everything else a route is. No new record, no new ranking idea, and no file in any repository: one store function decides a depot's effective routes, every reader of the match uses it, three route commands grow (a note, a per-depot list, a limited `route set`), one new command answers for a single bead, and packs learn to leave a stage to a default that already works it. Each choice below that was the operator's to confirm is marked as an ask in the text; the asks were put to mckean and are decided in section 5, which names the pick for each, and the proposal and beads are written for the picked options.

### 3.1 The data model: no depot means yard default

A route belongs to exactly one depot, named by its depot filter as today, or to no depot, and no depot now means yard default. The stored record does not change shape for scope: `Route.Depot` stays `*string`, nil is the default, and the words change around it (`yardr route list` gains a SCOPE column that prints the depot's name or `default`; the glossary's route line gains the sentence). Three shapes were considered:

- **No depot is the default (proposed).** The six depot-less routes are already yard defaults in behaviour — they match every depot — so nothing migrates in the store, no schema version moves, and a yard from before reads exactly as a yard after until someone states an own route that sets a default aside (section 3.2). The cost is that one field carries two readings across time: in a table from before, “no depot” meant “matches anywhere, ranked with everything”; after, it means “serves any depot that does not state its own”. The readings differ only where a depot states the same beads, which is the point of the change, and Decision 4 in section 5 confirms the dead routes that difference exposes.
- **An explicit scope field** (`scope: yard` beside the depot, or a depot named `*`). The stored truth is louder and an old binary cannot mistake a default for a match-anywhere route. The cost is a second field that must agree with the first forever (a route with both a depot and yard scope is nonsense the store must refuse), a migration of every stored route, and a pack and event format that learn the new field, to buy a distinction the resolution rule already makes.
- **A default is a route template depots copy.** Rejected outright: it is today's copies with a generator, and the copies drift again.

One field is added, for the folded-in note: `Note string` on `Route` (`json:"note,omitempty"`), written by `--note` on `route add` and by `route set` (section 3.5), carried by the `route_added` and `route_removed` events when set, and shown by `route list`. A route gets no name: its filters are its name in every view (`new, label difficulty:3, in yardr`), and a name would need a uniqueness rule, a rename, and a second way to refer to a route beside its id, for no reader who does not already read the filters. The note is where priority's meaning goes (section 3.3): `--note "P0/P1 reserve: strong group whatever the week says"` says what priority 5 alone cannot.

### 3.2 Ownership and resolution: an own route sets a default aside only for the beads it states

“The same stage and beads” is made exact as a route's **match key**: its stage, its type (unset is any type), its set of labels (order does not matter; the stored first label and the rest are one set), its bead priority (unset is any), and its originator resolved as `MatchesOriginator` resolves it (unset is the operator, `any` is every originator, a name is that user; a route that names the operator and one that names nobody therefore have the same key while that user is operator). The key deliberately leaves out the depot itself, the priority, the group chain with its quota conditions, and the note: those are what an override overrides.

A depot's **effective routes** are its own routes plus every yard default whose match key no own route states. One store function computes them — `EffectiveRoutes(routes, depot, operator)` beside `MatchRoute` — and resolution for a bead is then exactly today's, inside that one set:

1. Take the bead's depot's effective routes (a bead with no depot has the defaults alone; a route naming another depot never matches, as today).
2. Keep those that match the bead, as `MatchRoute` matches today: stage, type, labels, bead priority, originator.
3. Rank by priority, then by specificity, then — new, and only to make the order sayable — an own route before a default, then by id. Step 3's new leg can only be reached by two effective routes that match one bead at the same priority and specificity without stating the same key; section 3.3 makes the add of such a pair fail where the pair states the same key, and the id leg remains for the rest, now named in `route explain` instead of silent (section 3.7).
4. The winner's quota chain chooses the group as today (section 3.6).

The rejected reading of “a depot's own route wins” is that any own route for a stage sets aside every default for that stage, or that scope outranks priority across different keys. It breaks the estimate routes on contact: depot yardr states plain `new` routes, so a `difficulty:3` bead there would lose the depot-less `difficulty:3` route to `muse-wonder` and fall to the plain route — the label would stop meaning anything in every depot that routes its build stage at all, which is every depot. Under the match key, only a depot that states `new` with the label `difficulty:3` for the operator's beads sets that default aside, which is exactly a depot saying “hard beads here go elsewhere”. What this does to each cross-depot label shape:

- **Estimate labels stay defaults and keep working everywhere.** `difficulty:1`, `difficulty:2` and `difficulty:3` in `new`, and `difficulty:3` in `review-security`, are already depot-less; they remain defaults, and no depot's plain or harness-label routes share their key, so they rank by priority and specificity inside each depot's effective set as they do today. A depot that wants hard beads on its own pool adds one own route with the same key; the default leaves that depot's effective set and stays in every other depot's.
- **`claude` becomes a default; `muse` becomes a default with own overrides.** The `claude` routes name one group (`yardr-builders`) in every depot that has one, so one default serves them (yardr-hosted's copy spells `--originator mckean` where the others name nobody; the operator is mckean, so the keys agree, and the migration in section 3.8 folds it in — a route that must keep meaning mckean after an operator change would be stated with his name on purpose and would then be a different key). The `muse` routes name three pools, so the majority pool (`muse-light`, three depots) becomes the default and footplate, yardr and yardr-hosted each state an own `muse` route with the same key to their own pools; their effective sets lose the default and keep their answer. Harness labels are not made yard policy by this: the default is only the pool most depots already chose, and stating an own route is one command.
- **`brakeman-review` becomes a default.** Three identical copies (stage `review`, that label, originator `any`, priority 10, group `brakeman-reviews`) become one default; a depot that wants its security-labelled reviews elsewhere states the same key to another group. The label, not the brake, is what travels.

Priority stays the ranking's first leg, unchanged in meaning and now documented per route by its note. That a default at priority 3 can still outrank a depot's own plain route at priority 0 for beads both match (different keys) is kept on purpose: it is how the design-draft default works, and the alternative — scope before priority across keys — is the rejected reading above. A depot that wants its plain route to beat a labelled default gives the plain route the label's key or a higher priority, both visible in its effective list.

### 3.3 Ties: an exact tie is refused at the add

An **exact tie** is a new (or changed, section 3.5) route whose match key and priority equal a route of the same scope already standing — two yard defaults, or two own routes of one depot — while its group chain differs. (The same key, priority and chain is a duplicate, refused in the same words with “the same route”.) A default and an own route that state the same key can never tie: the own route sets the default aside in that depot (section 3.2), so the two are never in one depot's effective set together, and the own route's statement is the intended override, not a tie. The check is therefore same-scope: a new own route is checked against that depot's own routes, and a new default against the standing defaults. `AddRoute` refuses it, naming both routes and the one priority or key change that separates them:

```text
$ yardr route add --stage new --depot yardr --group 'yardr-builders@Weekly<target' --else muse-builders
route 57h28dx9dx9cmydnrzj9 already takes these beads in depot yardr at priority 0
(new, any type, no label, any bead priority, the operator's beads) to
yardr-builders@Weekly<target+10 else muse-builders. Two routes that take the
same beads at the same priority are ordered by id, which nobody chose.
Change one route's priority, change what beads it takes, or replace it:
yardr route set 57h28dx9dx9cmydnrzj9 --group 'yardr-builders@Weekly<target' --else muse-builders
```

Refusal, not a warning, was proposed and is decided (Decision 1, ask 6107): a warned-about dead route is what the yard has today (`y7qe3ems183zfz31md09` was addable, was added, and routed nothing), a warning on stderr is gone with the shell that printed it, and the fix is always at hand — the two routes take the same beads, so the operator means one of them, and `route set` or `route rm` plus the add says which. The check is over match keys, not over overlap in general: two routes with different keys that can both match one bead at the same priority and specificity (a `--type pr` route and a `--label` route, say) remain addable and are ranked own-before-default then by id, because whether they ever meet depends on beads not yet created, and refusing them would refuse working tables — signalbox's `review` routes are such a set. `route explain` names the id leg when it decides (section 3.7), so what remains silent today is at least sayable afterwards. Checking a default needs no per-depot sweep for ties: a default that states a key an own route already states is simply set aside in that depot (section 3.2) and stands everywhere else, which is the override working as designed; only a second default with the same key and priority is refused, and it is refused once, naming the standing default.

### 3.4 Stage names and flows: a default names no flow, pinned flows share routing, and the pull-request case is the proof

A default is not per flow and names no flow. The alternative — a flow filter on the route, or one default per flow name — was weighed and set aside, for a reason that needs stating exactly because the first draft of this section stated it wrong. A bead does carry a flow pin when it names one (`flow=<name>` in its metadata; `PinnedFlow` in `internal/store/flows.go`), and `resolveFlow` gives that pin precedence over the depot's and the type's pointers (`resolveFlow` in the same file; docs/flows.md, “Which flow governs a bead”), so `resolveFlow` returning one flow *for that bead* does not mean one flow governs a (depot, type) pair: two beads of the same depot and the same type can follow different flows that both name a stage `review`, one by pin and one by pointer. `MatchRoute` reads only the bead's stage, type, depot, labels, bead priority and originator, so a route — own or default — cannot tell those two beads apart, and reaches both. The decision taken here, and decided by mckean (Decision 6, ask 6109), is to accept that sharing: a pin chooses which stages and moves govern a bead, not who works a stage of that name, and routing stays blind to flows. Two beads in one depot, of one type, in one stage, one pinned to another flow and one not, therefore take the same winning route; an operator who needs a pinned flow's stage worked by a different group separates those beads by a means routing already reads — a label applied when the bead is pinned, or a distinct type, bead priority or originator — and states the own route or default for that key. Distinguishing flows in the match instead (a `--flow` filter, or the resolved flow name in the match key of section 3.2) was rejected: `MatchRoute` is a pure ranking over the routes and fields a caller already holds, called by the dispatcher, by `stageWork` (which re-stages a bead it holds), by the flows board's probe bead (`Bead{Stage, Type, Depot, Priority}` with no labels, no pin and no originator), by the ETA cells and by the web board, while resolving a bead's flow can need the depot's repository flows read outside the match; pins are the per-bead escape hatch of docs/flows.md, not a depot's main line, which the depot and type pointers already separate, and the gain — a separate pool for one pinned flow's same-named stage without a label — does not carry a flow resolution in every match. The pull-request case, which is the test the bead sets, covers only the different-type separation and keeps working: a default `review` route with no type (to the reviewers) matches a signalbox pull-request bead in stage `review`, because no type is any type; signalbox's own `review` route with `--type pr` (to `signalbox-pr-reviewers`) has a different match key, so both stand in signalbox's effective set; both match the bead, at the same priority the typed route is the more specific and wins, exactly as today. A depot whose pull requests should follow the yard's reviewers states nothing; a depot whose ordinary reviews should differ from its pull-request reviews keeps the typed own route it has. What a default on `review` does reach, deliberately, is every depot's `review` stage whatever flow named it — by pointer or by pin — that is what a yard default is for, and a depot whose `review` means something else states its own key. The flow view keeps its probe bead (no labels, default priority, the operator's beads, no pin; docs/groups.md) and now probes the depot's effective set, so `yardr flow show` for signalbox names `signalbox-pr-reviewers` for the pull-request flow's `review` and the default's group for the plain flow's, from one computation; a pinned bead following either flow takes the same route its probe names for that stage, which `route explain` states by naming the bead's pin beside the answer it does not change (bead 3 in section 6).

### 3.5 Editing a route: `route set` changes where beads go, never which beads

`yardr route set <id>` changes a route in place and keeps its id (Decision 2, ask 6108). It takes the spellings `route add` takes for what a route sends beads to — `--group` with its condition, repeatable `--else`, `--priority`, `--note` — and nothing else: stage, type, depot (which is now scope), labels, bead priority and originator are the route's identity (its match key), and changing them is removing one route and adding another, with the tie check and a new id honestly saying so. What is not named is kept, as `yardr group set` keeps a group's unnamed settings; a set that changes nothing writes nothing and logs nothing; a set that changes something logs one event with the route as it was and as it is, beside `route_added` and `route_removed` (docs/groups.md gains the row). In-flight truth is unchanged: a bead claimed through the route keeps its group until its session ends, and the new chain serves the next claim, which `bead show` already names by route id — an id that now survives the retune it describes. The two alternatives: a replace that keeps the id (`route add --replace <id>`, one atomic swap of the whole record) can change the key as well, which makes it a delete that lies about continuity — events and `bead show` lines would cite one id for two different routes; and neither (rm plus add forever) keeps the new-id churn and the two-route window that made duplicates, against a store that already has the patch shape in `SetGroup` and `GroupEdit` to follow. The pack's add-then-delete (section 3.8) is untouched: it replaces whole stages, key included, and a fresh id there is correct.

### 3.6 Originators, quota chains and permissions carry over

**Originators, unchanged.** A default takes the operator's beads unless it says otherwise, exactly as an own route does: nil is the operator, a name is that user, `any` is everyone, and `AddRoute` still stores `any` for a route whose first group starts no agents, which is why the `backlog` and `decide` defaults (manual groups) serve every originator without saying so, as their per-depot copies do today. Per-person routing keeps working because the originator is part of the match key: a route for one user's beads (to their personal group, or a subsidy route to the operator's pool) has a key no operator route states, so it is never set aside by one, and an own personal route sets aside only a default with that same user. The one spelling folded in by the migration (yardr-hosted's `--originator mckean` where the operator is mckean) is called out there, not normalised silently in code.

**Quota conditions and `--else` chains, unchanged.** The chain lives on the route record (`When`, `Else`), the key that decides shadowing excludes it, and `Choose` reads it exactly as today, whichever scope the route has: a default may carry a chain (the depot-less `review-security` route already does), a depot may set a default aside with the same key and a different chain, and the dispatcher remains the only reader that asks a provider, under the same five-minute memory, the same “cannot be evaluated holds”, the same one `quota_wait` event. No pack brings a condition or a chain, before or after (docs/groups.md), and deleting a group still removes every route that names it in any position.

**Permissions, unchanged but for what yardr-g7kq already brings.** Adding a route of either scope stays attribution-only with the pane-resolved actor, and removing one stays the operator's, extended by yardr-g7kq to the crew member the `yardmaster` setting names — for defaults and own routes alike, `route set` taking the add rule (anyone, attributed), since it cannot widen which beads a route takes. Making defaults the operator's alone to add was considered and decided against (Decision 3, ask 6110): depot setup and pack imports run as the yardmaster, the yardmaster already adds depot-less routes in the ordinary work of the yard (the six defaults are its work as much as the operator's), and a default's blast radius is better carried by the tie refusal, the effective list and `route explain`, which show the reach before and after, than by a gate that would route daily work through the operator or a run ask. The note is not a permission and is shown to anyone who may list routes, as the rest of the route is.

It is not enough by itself — it ranks routes, it does not try a bead against them — so one command more is built (Decision 5, ask 6112):


`route explain <bead>` is read-only and asks no provider: it shows the bead's matching facts, each effective route that matches with why it stands where it does (priority, specificity, own before default, id, labelled as such), the winner, the defaults set aside for this depot with their setters, and for a winning route with a quota chain the groups in order with “the choice among these is made at the claim, from the windows then” — the post-claim truth stays where it is, in `bead show` and the `quota_wait` event. It reuses `routeMatch`'s words for what matched and the effective-set computation, so the dispatcher, the explanation and the list cannot drift apart; for a bead with no depot it explains against the defaults. Nothing is written and no event is logged, as with `route list`.

### 3.8 Packs and depot setup after: a stage a default already works is left to it

The line a pack describes does not change, and a pack still carries no route: the planner's question for each stage becomes “what does this depot's effective set do with a plain bead of this stage (and type)?”, asked through the same `EffectiveRoutes`. If the winner is a plain route (one group, no condition) to the group the pack binds for the stage, the plan writes no route and says `stage new of flow default: worked by yard default <id> to <group>; no route written`. If the winner leads elsewhere, or no route wins, the plan writes the own route it writes today (same key as the stage's, so it sets the default aside for that depot) and replaces the depot's own routes for the stage as today. Routes that ask something of the single bead and routes for stages the pack lacks remain left alone, now including defaults of those shapes, each listed as not in the pack with its scope; a stage whose effective winner carries a quota chain is left to that chain and listed, the pack writing no route over a condition it cannot say. Export mirrors it: a stage the following route works through a default is exported with that group and the route listed as a yard default the pack follows, not left out. A fresh `yardr depot init` therefore writes own routes only where the depot's line differs from the yard's defaults — for the starter pack on this yard, the build, review and assembly stages whose pools are the depot's, and none for `backlog` or `decide` — and re-applying a pack to a migrated depot does not resurrect the copies the migration removed. What a pack import may never do is write or remove a default: imports are per depot, and a plan that finds it must change a default to fit says so and stops for a hand.

## 4. Left out

- **Depot tags or depot sets** (“these five depots share a route”): weighed in the conversation that asked for this design and set aside for routes in depots; nothing here needs a set, since a default plus per-depot overrides covers the observed copies, and a set would need membership, naming and a third scope.
- **Routes in repository files**, and any route a pack carries beyond the one stage route it has today: the question settles routes in the store, and section 2 gives the reason (a repository does not know this yard's groups or quota, and a contributor's commit must not reroute its own bead). Depot setup writes less (section 3.8) without packs learning what a default is.
- **Any change to groups, quota sources or the conditions' grammar**: the chain, its windows and its sources are yardr-xf5c's machinery, carried over whole (section 3.6). A default with a condition gates on each depot's beads alike because the windows are the group's provider's, not the depot's.
- **Named priorities or a priority scale** (small integers renamed, or levels): the numbers stay the operator's, ranked within one depot's effective set; the note (section 3.1) is where a priority's meaning is written, per route, instead of a yard-wide legend that labelled and reserve routes would strain.
- **Flow-scoped routes or defaults** (a flow name on a route, or the resolved flow in the match key): section 3.4 takes the sharing instead — the depot and type pointers already separate a depot's main lines, a bead's pin is an escape hatch that chooses stages and moves rather than a pool, and separating a pinned flow's stage, where it is ever needed, is done with a label or another field routing already reads.
- **A route name, renaming, or referring to routes by anything but id and filters**: section 3.1.
- **Editing which beads a route takes in place** (`route set` on stage, type, depot, labels, bead priority or originator): section 3.5; that is a different route, and the add's tie check must see it.
- **Automatic migration code** (a store version that rewrites routes, or an upgrade that collapses copies): the collapse is a one-time operation on one yard with judgements in it (which duplicate wins, which dead routes go), done with the new commands and checked by `route explain`; other yards, if any ever exist, state their own.
- **Tightening who may add or set routes beyond pane attribution**: Decision 3 in section 5 decides against it with reasons; the yardmaster's removal right is yardr-g7kq's, taken as given.

## 5. Decisions (the open questions, decided)

The questions this section held when drafted were put to mckean as asks on 16 Oct 2026 and are decided. Each entry keeps the options as drafted, the pick, and what the pick costs. The proposal and beads above and below are written for the picked options; where a different pick would have closed a bead, that is now history.

1. **An exact tie at the add: refused, or added with a warning?** (a) Refused, as section 3.3 writes it: no dead route ever stands, the error names the fix, and the yard's existing duplicate is the evidence that a warning does not prevent one. The cost is that a deliberate “standby” route at the same key and priority cannot be parked in the table; it is written with a note at a lower priority instead, where `route list --depot` shows it ranked and harmless. (b) Added with a warning on stderr, as `route add` warns today of a fallback on the same provider: nothing is ever blocked, and the warning is gone with the shell, the list shows two routes whose order only `route explain` explains, and the next duplicate is as easy as the last. I would take (a). **Decided: refused** (ask 6107, mckean picked 1). No dead route ever stands; a standby route goes in at a lower priority with a note.
2. **Editing in place: the limited `route set`, a whole-record replace that keeps the id, or neither?** (a) The limited set of section 3.5 (group chain, priority, note; identity by remove and add): most retunes — the group, the threshold, the rank — keep the id that events and `bead show` cite, and a change to which beads a route takes is visibly a new route. The cost is two ways to change a route, with the boundary to explain. (b) A replace that keeps the id over any field: one way, and an id that has named two different routes, which `route explain` and old `claimed` events then misdescribe. (c) Neither: today's rm plus add, with its new ids and two-route window, now softened by the tie refusal (the re-add after a removal cannot tie) but still churning the id every retune cites. I would take (a). **Decided: the limited `route set`** (ask 6108, mckean picked 1). Group chain, priority and note keep the id; which beads a route takes is a new route.
3. **Who may change a yard default: as routes today, or the operator's alone?** (a) As today for both scopes: add and set by anyone, recorded as the pane-resolved actor; remove by the operator or the yardmaster-setting crew member (yardr-g7kq). Depot setup and the yardmaster's daily work keep flowing, and the tie check, effective list and explain carry the blast radius. (b) Defaults the operator's alone to add, set and remove (own routes as today): a change that reaches every depot is one person's, at the cost of routing the yardmaster's ordinary default work through the operator or a run ask, and of a scope check beside every pack and setup path that today adds routes without asking which kind they are. I would take (a). **Decided: as routes today** (ask 6110, mckean picked 1). Add and set by anyone (recorded by pane-resolved actor), remove by the operator or the yardmaster-setting crew member (yardr-g7kq).
4. **The migration's judgement calls: confirm them?** (a) As section 3.9 writes them: yardr's `Weekly<target` duplicate is removed keeping `target+10` (today's winner), and yardr's and footplate's priority-0 `draft` routes are removed as dead, so design drafts keep going to `muse-wonder`; yardr-hosted's spelled-out `--originator mckean` folds into the operator's routes. No bead's winner changes. (b) Keep the `draft` routes as live own overrides: design drafts in yardr and footplate move to `muse-builders` at the cutover, which may be what their author meant and is a behaviour change this design has no evidence for. (c) Keep the spelled-out originator routes as a distinct key meaning mckean forever: one more key spelling for the same beads today, diverging only if the operator changes. I would take (a), each removal shown by `route explain` before and after in the migration bead. **Decided: confirm all three** (ask 6111, mckean picked 1). Nothing changes where a bead goes; the migration changes the table, not the routing.
5. **The preview: `route list --depot` alone, or `route explain <bead>` as well?** (a) Both, as section 3.7 writes them: the list answers “what governs this depot”, the explain answers “where would this bead go, and what did it pass over”, reusing one computation. The cost is one more command, read-only, over machinery the list already needs. (b) The list alone: less to build, and where a bead would go is still answered only afterwards, by claiming it — the gap the question names. I would take (a). **Decided: both** (ask 6112, mckean picked 1). The list says what governs a depot, `route explain` where one bead would go.
6. **Beads in one depot and of one type but on different flows (a pinned flow): may they share routes for a stage of the same name?** Raised by the critique (note 6097) and decided in section 3.4: a pin chooses which stages and moves govern a bead, not who works a stage of that name; routing reads stage, type, depot, labels, bead priority and originator, not the flow. (a) Share, separating by label when needed, with no flow in routing: no pinned bead routes differently today, and a label already separates them. (b) Add a flow filter to routes: exact, but one more key on every route and in every tie, and a flow resolution inside every match. (c) Leave it to yardmaster. I would take (a). **Decided: share** (ask 6109, mckean picked 1). Separate by label when needed; no flow in routing.
